The workflow

How to Turn 30-60 Minutes of Raw Footage Into Short Clips (Without Editing Experience)

If you've got a phone or camera full of long, unedited footage — a trip, a day of filming, a stream recording — and no real editing background, the usual advice ("just learn Premiere" or "just learn Final Cut") isn't actually helpful. Those tools have a real learning curve, and most people don't have hours to spend on it before they can post anything.

Here's the actual problem, plainly: long raw footage has dead air, boring stretches, and a handful of genuinely good moments buried inside it — and finding those moments by hand means watching the whole thing multiple times.

What actually needs to happen

  1. Remove the dead air — pauses, silence, nothing happening
  2. Find the moments actually worth keeping — the parts with real motion, real energy, real content
  3. Cut those into something short enough to post — 15-60 seconds, not 30-60 minutes
  4. Reframe it for wherever you're posting — vertical for Reels/TikTok, square for feed, landscape for YouTube

Doing all four of those by hand, in a traditional editor, is exactly the kind of thing that makes people give up before they ever post anything.

Why this is a common problem, not a niche one

This isn't just a "vlogger problem." Anyone who records long-form content and wants short clips out of it hits this same wall: podcasters, streamers, event videographers, anyone documenting a trip. The footage exists. The willingness to manually scrub through an hour of it looking for twenty good seconds usually doesn't.

What I'm building for this specific problem

I'm building Cutaway — a video editor built specifically around this exact workflow, not general-purpose professional editing. It automatically detects dead air, automatically scores footage for the most interesting moments, and turns a long file into a short one with a couple of clicks — including reframing for whichever platform you're posting to.

It's a personal project, built in public, from someone who had this exact problem and didn't want to learn a professional editor to solve it.

How to actually use it

Everything below matches the real app UI — no hidden menus, no settings buried three levels deep.

Cut the dead air

Open Pro Features → Cut dead air, click Scan clip. Once it finds the quiet stretches, a Cut button appears showing how many spots it found — click it to remove them.

Use a template

Open the Templates tab and pick Just clean it up if you want dead air removed automatically, keeping everything else as-is.

Random sampler

Its own tab, separate from Templates — pulls a genuinely different set of clips every time you run it, with no scoring or judgment involved, just variety. Good for generating fresh short-form content from the same long file without repeating yourself.

Trim, Split & Cut

Open the Trim / Split / Cut tab and choose a tool:

  • Trim only — drag the edges of the amber bar on the timeline to adjust start/end, or type an exact time into the Start/End fields for frame-accurate precision.
  • Split & Cut — click anywhere on the timeline to jump there, hit Split to cut at that point, then select a piece and hit Remove to delete it. Full Undo throughout.

Cuts stay visible on the timeline even after closing the tool, so you always know what's been removed.

Add text and captions

Open the + Text tab, click + Add text, then drag it into position directly on the video. Pick a font, toggle bold/italic, adjust size and color, and drag its bar on the timeline to set when it appears. Non-Latin scripts (tested with Korean) render correctly too.

Export in 4K, or reframe for any platform

Next to the Export button: choose an aspect ratio (9:16 for Reels/Shorts, 1:1 for feed, 16:9 for YouTube, or Original), and check 4K for true upscaled resolution.

Mix clips from multiple videos (VidBash)

Click 🎬 VidBash in the top bar. Import a few clips into the media pool, click one to preview it, use Set In / Set Out to trim the piece you want, then + Add to mix. Reorder pieces with the arrows, and Export mix when the sequence looks right — even across clips from completely different source videos.

Save your project

Save Project in the top bar saves every cut, every piece of text, and your export settings to a file. Open Project restores it exactly — close the app without losing your work.

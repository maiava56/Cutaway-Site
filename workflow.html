The workflow

How to Turn 30-60 Minutes of Raw Footage Into Short Clips (Without Editing Experience)

If you've got a phone or camera full of long, unedited footage — a trip, a day of filming, a stream recording — and no real editing background, the usual advice ("just learn Premiere" or "just learn Final Cut") isn't actually helpful. Those tools have a real learning curve, and most people don't have hours to spend on it before they can post anything.

Here's the actual problem, plainly: long raw footage has dead air, boring stretches, and a handful of genuinely good moments buried inside it — and finding those moments by hand means watching the whole thing multiple times.

What actually needs to happen

  1. Remove the dead air — pauses, silence, nothing happening
  2. Find the moments actually worth keeping — the parts with real motion, real energy, real content
  3. Cut those into something short enough to post — 15-60 seconds, not 30-60 minutes
  4. Reframe it for wherever you're posting — vertical for Reels/TikTok, square for feed, landscape for YouTube

Doing all four of those by hand, in a traditional editor, is exactly the kind of thing that makes people give up before they ever post anything.

Why this is a common problem, not a niche one

This isn't just a "vlogger problem." Anyone who records long-form content and wants short clips out of it hits this same wall: podcasters, streamers, event videographers, anyone documenting a trip. The footage exists. The willingness to manually scrub through an hour of it looking for twenty good seconds usually doesn't.

What I'm building for this specific problem

I'm building Cutaway — a video editor built specifically around this exact workflow, not general-purpose professional editing. It automatically detects dead air, automatically scores footage for the most interesting moments, and turns a long file into a short one with a couple of clicks — including reframing for whichever platform you're posting to.

It's a personal project, built in public, from someone who had this exact problem and didn't want to learn a professional editor to solve it.
